# After Hours content review

Reviewed 2026-10-24 (America/New_York). Source of truth: `src/lib/cah-after-hours.json`.

## Inventory and editorial scope

The pack contains 250 original one-blank prompts and 500 original response cards. Ten categories each contain 25 prompts and 50 responses: politics, history, celebrity, hypocrisy, power-abuse, mortality, religion, adult-health, dating, and dystopia. IDs are stable sequential `ah-p-001` through `ah-p-250` and `ah-r-001` through `ah-r-500`.

The comedy uses gallows humor, institutional cruelty, corruption, public hypocrisy, mortality, adult relationships, pregnancy, awkward STI appointments, abusive power, and historical disaster. Racism and sexism appear as the targets of satire. Abuse cards target perpetrators and institutional enablers. Religious cards satirize authority, money, supernatural bureaucracy, and fictional situations. The pack does not advocate hatred of protected groups, celebrate sexual abuse, depict sexual conduct involving minors, or make medical claims about real people.

Cards were individually written rather than generated by substituting words into combinatorial templates. Familiar comic structures recur (bureaucracy, subscriptions, accountability), but each card has its own situation. An editorial pass replaced eleven repeated or weak premises, including a second ancestry-test joke, a second explosive gender-reveal joke, and several overlapping pregnancy-planning jokes. The repeated settings support combinations; they do not establish that every combination is funny.

A second requested tone pass revised six prompts and twenty-two responses. Three additional named public figures replaced generic celebrity material. Adult and mortality revisions add more explicit profanity, sexual embarrassment, STI disclosure awkwardness, pregnancy anxiety, funeral disasters, and tasteless gallows humor. All sexual situations concern adults; none depicts sexual assault or sexual acts involving a corpse. These revisions preserve stable IDs and category counts.

Historical cards use broad established settings (Titanic, Roman Empire, plague doctors, feudalism, colonialism, Cold War) in overtly impossible or fictional modern juxtapositions. They do not assert newly discovered facts, casualty counts, or crimes by an unverified real person. Named-person templates should retain their fictional framing; adding a real person's name to generic crime/health jokes requires a new review.

## Verification evidence

An automated Python check loaded the final JSON and asserted exact counts, sequential IDs, ten category counts, unique normalized text (case and punctuation removed), nonempty text, one `____` per prompt and none per response, and maximum 180 characters for every card. Result after the second tone pass: all assertions passed; longest prompt 83 characters, longest response 78 characters. No runtime dependencies were added.

A token-overlap scan compared every pair within prompts and responses after dropping common stop words; no pairs reached Jaccard overlap 0.5. This is a lexical screen, not proof of semantic originality. A separate manual premise review removed close concepts missed by that screen.

A seeded twelve-pair sample (`random.seed(24)`) exercised cross-category combinations. All cards can be inserted as noun phrases; some combinations are deliberately absurd. Sample pairs included `ah-p-172` + `ah-r-007` (the angel at the gate / senator's emotional support billionaire), `ah-p-056` + `ah-r-146` (comeback tour / confession edited into a perfume advertisement), and `ah-p-043` + `ah-r-371` (archaeologist / new parent's grocery-store stare). Sampling provides a limited editorial check; it does not validate all 125,000 possible pairs or human humor preferences.

This review covers static content. It does not establish multiplayer privacy, live card dealing, AI judge fairness, production deployment, age verification, or browser layout quality. Those require the engine, interface, and release work packages.
